## When to use this, and when not to

Use it if:

- You sell something worth a conversation, typically from a few hundred dollars up.
- You run your own calls, or you're the founder and the best salesperson right now.
- You want a repeatable process rather than winging each call.

Don't use it for:

- Self-serve, low-price products that should sell from a page without a call.
- Regulated selling such as financial products, insurance or medical services, where what you can say is governed by rules this pack doesn't cover.
- Selling something you don't believe solves the buyer's problem. No process fixes that.

## What it costs

Nothing beyond what you likely have. A Claude or ChatGPT account (free tiers work; paid tiers handle long call transcripts better), a spreadsheet for the pipeline, and optionally a meeting recorder that produces transcripts. Many video tools (Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams) offer transcription on some plans; check yours. Always tell people when a call is recorded, and get consent where the law requires it. In many places that means everyone on the call.

## Three rules for using AI in sales

1. **AI preps, you decide.** Use AI to research, summarize and draft. Never let it send anything to a prospect without you reading it.
2. **Never put private data where it shouldn't go.** Don't paste a client's confidential documents into an AI tool unless your agreement with them and the tool's data terms allow it. When in doubt, anonymize.
3. **No invented facts.** If AI research says the prospect "recently raised $10M", check the source before you mention it. Getting a fact wrong about someone's business on a sales call costs more trust than knowing nothing.
