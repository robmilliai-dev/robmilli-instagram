## Try it now: 15 minutes, no code

1. Open a daily chart of one stock in [TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/chart/) (free).
2. Add two indicators: **EMA 20** and **EMA 50**. (Click "Indicators," type EMA.)
3. Write down: today's price, the highest price of the last 20 days, the EMA 20 and EMA 50 numbers.
4. Open [Claude](https://claude.ai/) and paste this, filled in:

```text
You are my AI trading assistant. You never place trades. I decide.

Stock: [TICKER]
Today's price: [ ]
Highest price of the last 20 days: [ ]
EMA 20: [ ]   EMA 50: [ ]
Volume today vs normal: [e.g. "about 1.5x normal" or "don't know"]
My account: $[10,000]. I risk 1% per trade.

1. SETUP: Is this a BREAKOUT (price above the 20-day high on strong volume),
   a PULLBACK (EMA 20 above EMA 50, price dipped near EMA 20 and bounced),
   or NONE?
2. PLAN: If there's a setup, give me entry, stop (where I'm wrong) and target.
   The target must be at least 2x as far as the stop.
3. SIZE: How many shares so that hitting my stop only loses 1% of my account?
4. DECISION: APPROVE FOR REVIEW, WATCHLIST or REJECT.
   Give me the #1 reason NOT to take it.
Use only my numbers. Plain English. Under 120 words.
```

**REJECT will be the most common answer.** That's good. It means the AI is protecting your money.

## How the automatic version works

The real AI trader does the same thing, on its own, every hour:

1. **Scan.** It pulls prices for every stock on your watchlist. Free data.
2. **Spot.** It looks for the two setups: breakout or pullback.
3. **Plan.** It works out the entry, stop and target.
4. **Risk check.** It sizes the trade so a loss only costs 1%. If the reward is too small, it's a hard **no**. The AI can't argue past this.
5. **Decide.** Claude writes one short note: approve, watch or reject, and why.
6. **Text you.** The note goes straight to your phone. Every idea is saved in a log.

**To build it yourself,** you'd write a Python script for each step, connect a free price feed (like `yfinance`), set up a Telegram bot for texts, and schedule it to run hourly. If you know Python, that's a few evenings. If not, it's a lot longer.
