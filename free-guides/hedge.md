# The One-Ticker Committee

**Big hedge funds never let one person buy a stock alone.** They make a team argue about it first. One looks at the economy. One reads the company's numbers. One reads the chart. One hunts for risk. One argues the other side. A boss makes the call.

Now you can run that same team on any stock. Tonight. Free.

**When you're done, you'll have:**

- ✅ Six AI experts who argue about your stock
- ✅ The strongest reason **not** to buy, before you spend a dollar
- ✅ One clear call: **PASS**, **WATCH**, or **CANDIDATE**

**How it works:** One stock + one dated fact → 6 experts argue → the Devil's Advocate attacks → Risk can veto → PASS, WATCH or CANDIDATE

> **READ FIRST:** It never buys or sells. Most ideas get PASS or WATCH, and that's the point.

**Why this works:** One brain falls in love with an idea. A team with a skeptic doesn't. When an AI has to argue against itself, the weak ideas die early. That's the whole trick, and you'll see it happen in step 3.

All you need: [Claude](https://claude.ai/) and one stock you're curious about.

## Step 1: Pick one stock and one fact (4 minutes)

1. Pick a stock. For example: **MSFT**.
2. Find one recent, dated fact about it. Easy places: the company's investor page, or [SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/) (search the company, open the latest report).
3. Write it down like this: *"MSFT, Oct 2026 quarterly report, page 3: revenue grew 15%."*

No fact handy? That's OK. Write "none." The team will tell you what's missing.

## Step 2: Build your committee (3 minutes)

1. In [Claude](https://claude.ai/), make a new **Project** called **My Committee**. (No Projects? Use a new chat.)
2. Paste this into the Project instructions:

```text
You are a 6-person stock research committee. You never buy, sell or tell me to trade.

The team:
1. MACRO: what's going on in the economy that matters for this stock?
2. FUNDAMENTALS: what do the company's own numbers say?
3. CHART: what does the price trend say? (Say "no chart given" if I gave none.)
4. RISK MANAGER: what could go badly wrong? You can VETO.
5. DEVIL'S ADVOCATE: make the best case AGAINST this idea.
6. BOSS: weigh it all and decide: PASS, WATCH or CANDIDATE.
   PASS and WATCH mean don't buy. CANDIDATE means it's worth a closer look.
   If Risk Manager vetoes, the answer can't be CANDIDATE.

Rules: Only use facts I give you. Never make up numbers, news or dates.
If something is missing, say "missing" and let it count against the idea.
Each person gets 3-4 short lines. Plain English.
End with: the call, the #1 reason against, and what would change your mind.
```

## Step 3: Bring the stock to the committee (5 minutes)

Send this as your next message:

```text
Stock: [TICKER]
Today's date: [DATE]
My idea in one line: [e.g. "I think they're growing faster than people expect"]
Fact I found: [YOUR FACT, with date and where you found it, or "none"]
Price chart: [attach a screenshot, or "none"]

Run the committee.
```

## Step 4: Read the "no" first (3 minutes)

1. Read the **Devil's Advocate** first. Can you answer that argument? If not, your answer is WATCH.
2. Check the **Boss's call**. Most of the time it's PASS or WATCH. That's normal. It means the team saved you from a weak idea.
3. Save the call, the #1 reason against, and a date to look again.

**You did it if:** you have one committee call saved, with the best argument against it.

> Educational only. Not financial advice. Six AI roles are still one AI, so check every fact yourself.

## Make it yours: your investing rules (3 minutes)

Add this to your committee Project so every member judges stocks against **your** plan:

```text
MY INVESTING RULES
I hold for: ____ (weeks / months / years)
Max in one stock: 10% of my money
I never buy: ____ (e.g. no profit yet, too much debt, sectors I don't understand)
I need: a dated reason to own it, a price where I'm wrong, and a reason it's not already priced in.
Final call options: PASS, WATCH or CANDIDATE. The Risk Manager can veto anything.
```

## 12 copy-paste prompts

**Finding ideas**

1. "Give me 5 stocks in [sector] the committee should look at, with one dated reason each. Use linked sources only."
2. "Here's my watchlist. Which ONE deserves a full committee meeting this week, and why?"

**The meeting**

3. "Run the full committee on [ticker] using this dated fact: [paste]. Every member speaks once, Risk last."
4. "Devil's Advocate only: give me the 3 strongest reasons this stock goes down from here."
5. "Macro only: what in rates, the dollar or the economy helps or hurts this stock right now?"
6. "Fundamental only: is the business getting better or worse? Use the last 4 quarters if I paste them."
7. "Technical only: here's the chart. Is now a good time, or is it better to wait for a pullback?"
8. "Risk Manager: veto or allow? If allowed, max size and the price where we admit we're wrong."

**Keeping score**

9. "Write a one-page memo: the idea, the case against, the call, and the date to check back."
10. "It's 30 days later, here's the price and news: [paste]. Was the committee right? Who was most right?"
11. "Look at my last 10 committee calls. Which member is most often right? Should I trust them more?"
12. "Compare [ticker A] vs [ticker B]. The committee can only pick one. Which, and why?"

## Weekly review (10 minutes, every Sunday)

This is where you actually get better. Paste your week's notes into Claude with this:

```text
Here are my notes from this week: [paste]
1. Which ideas worked and which didn't? Look for patterns (setup, day, time, market).
2. Where did I break my own rules?
3. Give me ONE change to make next week. Just one.
Use only what's in my notes. Don't guess.
```
