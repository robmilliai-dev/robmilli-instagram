# The 15-Minute Market Watch

**Stop staring at charts all day.** Pros don't. They pick a price, set an alert, and walk away. The market taps them on the shoulder when it's time.

In 15 minutes, you'll set that up too. Free.

**When you're done, you'll have:**

- ✅ An alert that pings your phone or email when your stock hits your price
- ✅ An AI "second opinion" that checks the move before you act
- ✅ A simple yes/no answer: **WORTH A LOOK** or **SKIP**

**How it works:** Pick a stock and a price → TradingView alert → your phone pings → AI checks the move → you write down what happened

> **READ FIRST:** The alert only tells you the price was hit. AI checks the move. You make every decision.

**Why this works:** Most bad trades happen when you chase a move you saw by accident. An alert flips that. You decide your price when you're calm. Then the AI checks the move with fresh eyes. You'll see it work on your own screen in step 3.

All you need: a free [TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/chart/) account and a free [Claude](https://claude.ai/) or ChatGPT account.

## Step 1: Pick your stock and your price (3 minutes)

1. Open [TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/chart/).
2. Type in a stock you like. For example: **AAPL**.
3. Look at the chart. Find a price you care about. Maybe it's the recent high. Maybe it's a price where it bounced before.
4. Write it down in one line. Example: *"AAPL at $235. That's the recent high."*

That's your line in the sand.

## Step 2: Set the alert (4 minutes)

1. Click the **clock icon** (Alert) at the top of the chart. Shortcut: **Alt+A** on Windows, **Option+A** on Mac.
2. Set it to: **[your stock] Crossing [your price]**.
3. Name it something simple, like **AAPL 235**.
4. Turn on **Notify in app** and **Send email**.
5. Click **Create**.

Done. TradingView now watches the price for you, all day, even when your laptop is closed.

## Step 3: When it goes off, ask AI to check it (5 minutes)

When your alert fires, open [Claude](https://claude.ai/) and paste this. Fill in the brackets.

**No alert yet?** Practice now with this made-up example: *stock AAPL, alert price $235, price now $236.10, reason "recent high".*

```text
You are my trading checker. Never tell me to buy or sell. I decide.

My alert just went off:
- Stock: [TICKER]
- My alert price: [PRICE]
- Price now: [PRICE]
- Date and time: [DATE, TIME]
- Why I set this alert: [ONE SENTENCE]

1. In plain words, what just happened?
2. Give me 3 reasons this could be a real move.
3. Give me 3 reasons this could be a fake-out.
4. What 2 things should I check on the chart before I do anything?
5. Your call: WORTH A LOOK or SKIP. One line why.

Only use what I told you. If you need more info, ask me.
Never make up prices or news.
```

## Step 4: Write down what happened (3 minutes)

Keep a simple note: the stock, the date, the AI's call, and what you did.

After 10 alerts, read your notes. You'll spot your own patterns fast. Most people find they were chasing moves they should have skipped.

**You did it if:** you have one live alert set, and one AI check saved in your notes.

> Educational only. Not financial advice. An alert is a heads-up, not a reason to buy.

## Make it yours: your watch rules (3 minutes)

Paste this at the top of your AI chat, filled in, so every alert is judged the same way:

```text
MY WATCH RULES
Watchlist (5 max): ____
Account size: $____   Max risk per idea: 1%
An alert only matters if: price CLOSES past my level (not just pokes through),
volume is above its 20-day average, and nothing big (earnings, Fed day) is due in 2 days.
For every alert, answer: STILL VALID, WAIT, or IGNORE, with one reason.
Never tell me to buy or sell. I decide.
```

## 12 copy-paste prompts

**Setting alerts**

1. "Here's my watchlist and a chart of each. Give me ONE alert price per stock and why that price matters."
2. "Which of these levels is most likely to be a fake move? Should I alert on the close instead?"
3. "Write the TradingView alert message I should use so it tells you the ticker, price, time and timeframe."

**When an alert fires**

4. "Alert fired: [paste]. Check it against my watch rules. STILL VALID, WAIT or IGNORE?"
5. "Here's the chart right now. Did price close past the level, or just poke through?"
6. "Is there earnings, a Fed meeting or big news in the next 2 days for this stock? Use dated sources only."
7. "If this is real, where is the idea proven wrong? That's my stop. How many shares at 1% risk on $___?"

**After**

8. "Here are the last 10 alerts and what happened after each. Which alert prices worked best?"
9. "Which stock on my list keeps giving false alerts? Should I drop it?"
10. "Rewrite my watch rules so I get fewer, better alerts."
11. "It's a quiet day. Give me a practice alert to rehearse the routine."
12. "Turn this week's alert log into 3 lessons."

## Your daily routine

| When | Do this |
|---|---|
| Morning (2 min) | Check your 5 alert prices still make sense. Move any that are stale. |
| When it pings (3 min) | Paste the alert into Claude with prompt 4. Note the answer. |
| After the close (2 min) | Write down what each alert did after it fired. |

## Weekly review (10 minutes, every Sunday)

This is where you actually get better. Paste your week's notes into Claude with this:

```text
Here are my notes from this week: [paste]
1. Which ideas worked and which didn't? Look for patterns (setup, day, time, market).
2. Where did I break my own rules?
3. Give me ONE change to make next week. Just one.
Use only what's in my notes. Don't guess.
```

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| The alert never fired | Check it's set to "Crossing" and the app has notifications on. |
| Too many alerts | Use one alert per stock, on prices that matter. |
| The AI check is vague | Paste the chart screenshot and the news headline with it. |
